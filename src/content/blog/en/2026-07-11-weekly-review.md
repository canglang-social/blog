Week 2 of my four-week build sprint had two main goals: apply to one
job every day, and ship my agent project. The honest score: one
application, one shipped project. The application count looks like a
failure until you see the dependency — my recruiter session and I had
agreed that for agent-engineer roles, a resume without a shipped agent
project isn't credible. So the ship gated the applications,
deliberately. As of this week, the gate is open.

learn-to-ship went from in-progress to public-with-a-live-demo in one
week, 71 commits. The mechanism that kept it moving while everything
else waited: QUESTIONS.md-driven development — every open question
becomes one small PR. The work was always small enough to finish, so it
never stalled. That's the practice I'm carrying to the next project.

The quieter win was environmental. A mid-week experiment — go out to a
café every morning — turned out to fix two things at once: it skips the
noon nap, and skipping the nap fixed my night sleep. I only noticed the
causal chain while compiling this review, which is the point of
reviewing at all.

Compiling the review surfaced something else: I could recall the things
I shipped, but not the infrastructure that improved underneath me — my
daily-review prompt versioned five times this week, my job tracker grew
a dashboard, and none of it surfaced from memory until the commit log
showed me. Review is output-driven; I'm practicing running my life
review-first.

Next week: Kubernetes, one gap skill at a time, and one application
every day — this time protected by a morning orient where the
application is task #1, before any build work. Spoken English moves to
lifestyle slots (work breaks, shows over lunch): the disagreement with
my recruiter about it dissolved once we saw we were arguing about
timing, not importance.
